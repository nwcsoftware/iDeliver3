The finding

The operation is viable on its own revenue. In 54 days it moved 6,301 orders — 116.69 a day — and kept USD 33,784.85 of the USD 114,842.37 it handled. That is USD 19,044.65 a month, or USD 228,360.56 annualised at the current run-rate, against a platform cost of USD 600 a year: the software is 0.26% of revenue.

The business breaks even while the all-in cost of making a delivery stays under USD 5.36. At 13 active drivers each completing about 8.88 deliveries a day, that is the number to manage.

Money handled versus money kept

Most of what the business touches is not its own. Fee coverage

6,044 of 6,301 orders carry a delivery fee. The remaining 257 (4.08%) went out with none — free deliveries, waived charges, or a fee left blank at entry. At the average of USD 5.36 that is USD 1,377.52 of foregone revenue over 54 days.

What the database cannot tell us

The software records every order, fee and collection, so the revenue side of this study is measured, not estimated. It does not record what the operation costs — driver pay, fuel, phones, rent, office salaries. What would move the numbers most

  1. Level the fleet. The busiest driver does several times the work of the quietest. Bringing the bottom half up to the median is capacity already paid for.
  2. Move orders off the call centre. 4.13% of orders arrive through the app or the portal today. Every point moved is staff time returned.
  3. Collect the commission. 98.43% of it is still outstanding; the mechanism exists and is not being used.
  4. Decide the free deliveries. 4.08% of orders carry no fee — worth roughly USD 776.51 a month.

Method

Every figure was read directly from the operating database on 23/08/2026, covering orders created between 01/07/2026 and 23/08/2026 inclusive — 54 days. 6,301 orders were examined, together with 938 packages, the retail invoices raised against them, and 5,965 payment collections. No sampling, and no projection except where a line is explicitly labelled as annualised or a per-month run-rate.

Revenue means what the business keeps: delivery fees, shop commission and advertising. Goods, packages and shop invoices are customers’ money in transit and are excluded from it — they appear only as “handled”. Cancelled and failed orders are excluded from volume and revenue. LBP is converted at 89,500 LBP/USD, stated here because the system does not store a rate; every LBP-denominated figure in this study depends on it. Costs other than the platform subscription are not recorded by the system and are modelled as a range rather than asserted.
